---

## Cars — rent-to-own options

### 1. Action Rent To Own — Orlando (local)
- No credit check; needs license, proof of income/address, liability insurance.
- Typical: ~$600–641/mo, $1,800–2,000 program fee, 48-month term.
- Early purchase option after 2 years; ownership transfers after payoff.
- Pro: local, same-day drive-off, no credit check. Con: total paid far exceeds the car's value; return early = lose everything paid.

### 2. DriveItAway — lease-to-own (national)
- Accepts any credit history; qualifies mainly on payment-to-income ratio.
- $450–1,000/mo depending on vehicle; insurance separate; purchase credits accrue toward buyout.
- Pro: flexible terms, purchase credits. Con: high monthly cost + separate insurance; verify per-vehicle terms.

### 3. Rent To Own Auto Centers
- No credit check; payment tailored to budget; bundles tax, tags, warranty, roadside assistance; can return the car anytime, never "upside-down."
- Pro: bundled costs, walk-away option. Con: confirm an Orlando location exists before pursuing.

### 4. Buy-here-pay-here dealers (fallback)
- In-house financing, minimal credit requirements, often weekly payments.
- Pro: accessible. Con: very high interest (often 20%+), GPS kill-switches, predatory terms — read every line.

**Car gotchas:** upfront program fees are non-refundable; missed payments can void the deal and forfeit all prior payments; "no credit check" always means higher total cost.

## Housing — rent-to-own options (Orlando area)

### 1. Dream America — serves Orlando
- Lease-option; 500 FICO min; needs $4,000/mo household funds and $5,000–8,000 cash; 1% onboarding fee; 10% of on-time rent becomes closing credit.
- 1-year lease; renew = rent AND purchase price both rise 5%. Buy within 6 months = price +6%; months 6–12 = +10%.
- Pro: operates in Orlando, lenient on credit. Con: needs thousands in cash; price escalators punish slow buyers; 2+ late payments = no renewal, possible eviction.

### 2. Landis — serves Florida
- Buys a home you pick within approved budget; 2–3% upfront ($5,000–7,500 on a $250k home); includes credit/savings coaching.
- Pro: choose your home, coaching included. Con: thousands upfront; budget capped by verified finances.

### 3. JAAG Properties
- Min 3% deposit fully credited to purchase; 24–48 month terms; you pick the home with a realtor, they buy it.
- Pro: deposit counts toward purchase. Con: 3% of Orlando prices ($300k+) = $9,000+.

### 4. Private lease-option (via local realtor/attorney)
- Negotiate directly with a seller: option fee typically 1–5% of price, part of monthly rent credited toward purchase, price locked upfront.
- Pro: negotiable terms. Con: unregulated — use a real estate attorney; if you don't buy, you lose the option fee and rent credits.

### 5. Best low-cash path: FHA + Florida assistance
- FHA: 3.5% down at 580+ FICO (10% down at 500–579); 100% of down payment can be gift or assistance funds.
- Florida Hometown Heroes: up to $35,000 down-payment assistance, 0% interest, forgivable after 30 years (needs 640 FICO, first-time buyer, full-time FL W-2 worker; funds periodically exhaust).
- Florida Assist: up to $10,000 deferred second mortgage, 0%, no monthly payment (repaid on sale/refinance).
- Orange County/Orlando local homebuyer assistance ~$8,000.
- Pro: lowest true cash-to-close; assistance can cover most of the down payment. Con: still requires mortgage qualification — income documentation, credit work, patience.

**Housing gotchas:** know lease-OPTION (right to buy) vs lease-PURCHASE (obligation to buy); upfront option fees are almost always non-refundable; verify the company actually owns the property; never pay fees before reviewing the signed contract with an attorney; rent credits vanish if you walk away.
